# Competitive landscape — 2026-09-02

## Scope and conclusion

This is a bounded comparison of official or primary public materials available on 2026-09-02. It is not an exhaustive survey, an independent reproduction of vendor results, or a novelty opinion.

The most important update is [Elicit's BioDecisionBench](https://elicit.com/blog/biodecisionbench-evals), published on 2026-08-04. Its 40 task variants, drawn from 26 life-science reasoning failures, evaluate evidence-grounded decisions from target selection through trial design and indication selection. Its rubrics score research process as well as answer quality, including evidence lineage, confounding, decision thresholds, risks, limitations, and recommendations. That is **material overlap** with CausalFrontier's broad problem framing. BioDecisionBench invalidates any novelty claim based merely on “benchmarking biomedical decisions,” reasoning over fragmented evidence, or evaluating what a biomedical team should do next.

Two systems published in *Nature* on 2026-05-19 sharpen the boundary further. [Co-Scientist](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10644-y) generates, critiques, ranks, and refines hypotheses and research proposals; its paper reports expert-in-the-loop evaluation and selected biomedical laboratory validations. [Empirical Research Assistance (ERA)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10658-6) generates and iteratively improves empirical software after a problem has been made scorable with a quality metric and evaluation data. These are distinct operating points, not cleanly disjoint product categories: Co-Scientist can propose experiments, and ERA can generate methodological ideas within a scored search.

An official [Google DeepMind policy analysis](https://deepmind.google/public-policy/conjecture-machines-ai-agents-and-the-new-validation-bottleneck-in-science/) frames the emerging constraint as validation: agents can make hypotheses and candidate solutions cheaper and more abundant while empirical refutation remains slow, costly, physical, and institutional. That framing motivates better triage and audit before scarce validation resources are spent, but it is not evidence that CausalFrontier supplies either capability.

CausalFrontier's narrower hypothesis remains unvalidated: **machine-verifiable pre-outcome commitments to evidence, actions, complete outcome branches, scoring, and abstention; independent custody and reveal; and measured real time and cost to the next decision-relevant falsification.** The hypothesis is the conjunction of these properties and prospective baseline comparisons, not a claim that any component is new.

## Claims this landscape rules out

CausalFrontier must not claim novelty merely because it:

- benchmarks biomedical decisions, evidence synthesis, or recommendations about what to do next;
- uses a multi-agent system, persistent context, literature grounding, or iterative debate to generate and rank scientific hypotheses or experimental proposals;
- generates executable scientific methods and optimizes them against a predefined empirical score;
- presents realistic expert scientific tasks or grades reasoning with detailed rubrics;
- withholds test items, prevents contamination, or uses blind independent assessment;
- prospectively compares computational predictions with experimental outcomes; or
- measures time, efficiency, or cost in a scientific workflow.

BioDecisionBench is the closest direct overlap in decision scope. Co-Scientist establishes a major precedent for literature-grounded hypothesis generation, multi-agent refinement, experimental planning, persistent context, and selected expert-led empirical validation. ERA establishes a major precedent for searching scientific method space with executable software once an evaluation score exists. LifeSciBench, FrontierScience, LAB-Bench and BixBench establish substantial precedents for realistic scientific-work evaluation. CACHE and CASP establish strong precedents for prospective experimental and blind outcome evaluation. The OpenAI deployment reports establish precedents for first-party time and cost measurements. No novelty or exhaustiveness claim survives from any one of those properties.

## Still-unvalidated distinct hypothesis

The working boundary is therefore **pre-scoring audit**, not “AI that does science.” Co-Scientist addresses hypothesis generation and proposal refinement; ERA optimizes empirical software after a scorable objective and evaluation data exist. CausalFrontier V2 instead proposes to audit the earlier translation from fragmented, cutoff-bounded evidence into an admissible next falsifiable action, complete outcome branches, and abstention before outcome access. This is a provisional framing inferred from the reviewed public materials, not demonstrated uniqueness. The categories overlap, and V2 has not yet shown that its proposed audit layer is useful, reliable, or better than simpler controls.

The remaining research question should be frozen narrowly:

> Given a prospectively frozen evidence cutoff, can a system (1) byte-bind the admissible evidence, candidate actions, complete outcome branches, abstention rules, and scoring before outcome access; (2) use independent custody and reveal to make leakage, omission, and rollback auditable; (3) select the next decision-relevant falsification or abstain; and (4) reduce fully loaded real time and cost to that falsification by at least 10× against predeclared expert, retrieval, graph, random, and simple-rule baselines in each of at least three domains, while preserving calibrated coverage/selective risk, provenance, privacy, authority boundaries, independent reproduction, and early-career usability?

This is a testable conjunction, not a novelty claim. Its distinctiveness, feasibility, effect size, generality, and scientific value are all unvalidated. A structural repository, synthetic fixture, retrospective success, or rubric score cannot establish it.

## Consequences for the next evaluation

1. Treat BioDecisionBench as a direct comparator and, once its public artifacts are released, as a source of failure modes and baseline tasks. Report Elicit's results as vendor-authored until independently reproduced.
2. Treat Co-Scientist as a hypothesis-generation and experimental-planning comparator and ERA as a direct comparator for optimization after a task is scorable. Do not relabel either system as a CausalFrontier component without a direct protocol-level comparison.
3. Do not optimize for answer quality alone. Freeze machine-readable evidence admissibility, action candidates, total branches, abstention, and scoring before revealing outcomes.
4. Run the positive, failed-translation, and ambiguous controls as a set. A confident answer on the positive control is not sufficient evidence of calibration.
5. Use an independent party or service for outcome custody and reveal, and publish verifiable precommitment receipts before scoring.
6. Record fully loaded analyst time, compute, external-service cost, and elapsed time to the next decision-relevant falsification for every baseline. Do not infer acceleration from task accuracy.
7. Require selective-risk and coverage reporting so that abstention can beat unsafe confidence rather than being treated as a missing answer.
8. Update this comparison when BioDecisionBench's dataset, rubrics, splits, judging protocol, and independent evaluations become public. Until then, do not infer undocumented capabilities or deficiencies.

## Source and interpretation limits

The comparison uses official project pages, the primary *Nature* papers for Co-Scientist and ERA, and one primary peer-reviewed CACHE roadmap. Reported task counts, performance, speed, and validation results are the source authors' figures. This review did not reproduce benchmark scores, inspect Co-Scientist or ERA source code, audit hidden sets, independently verify laboratory results, search every commercial or academic system, or make a patent-law novelty determination. “Distinct hypothesis” means a narrow proposition still worth testing, not demonstrated uniqueness.
